Privacy policy

Last updated October 27, 2026

ooooh is an iPhone app made by me, Sean Reid. This page says what the app does with your words, where they go, and what I can and can't see. It's short because there isn't much to tell.

What I collect

Nothing that identifies you. The app has no accounts. You never give it a name, an email address, or a password. The device is the identity: Apple's App Attest proves to my server that a request comes from a real copy of the app on a real iPhone, and StoreKit proves whether that device has a subscription.

My server keeps a few records per device. Each one is keyed on the App Attest key identifier and nothing else. The key identifier is a random value Apple issues to your copy of the app. I can't turn it back into you. The records are:

  • The public key your phone attested, with a counter that moves forward on every signed request. This record has no expiry. If you delete the app, the next install attests a new key, and the old record stays behind as a public key tied to nothing.
  • The month's count of free sessions and the minutes they ran, kept for 62 days.
  • The open session, so a second start while one is running counts nothing. It expires four hours after the start. Beside it, the start time of the last session whose notes were written, so a second notes request for the same session is refused. The next session's notes replace it.
  • What the device spent on each provider today: Jev tokens, Claude tokens, and ElevenLabs characters, by UTC day. My server refuses a device past its daily cap until midnight UTC. Earlier days are dropped as the device spends on a new day.
  • The subscription record, if the device has one: the product id, the original transaction id, the account token, when Apple signed the transaction, when it expires, when its grace period ends, a revocation date if Apple revoked it, and when my server verified it. The account token is derived from the key identifier, so it names an install, not a person. The record is kept until 30 days after the subscription expires, alongside one note under the original transaction id saying which key identifier first claimed it, kept for the same time.
  • A count of signed requests in the current minute, replaced by the next minute's. The two calls that enrol a key happen before there is a key identifier, so those are counted per IP address instead, gone after two minutes.
  • A one-time challenge for enrolling a key: 32 random bytes tied to no device, deleted on use or after five minutes.

None of this holds a name, an email address, an Apple ID, audio, or a transcript, and nothing in it links a key identifier to a person. Every signed request also carries the time it was signed. My server checks that the time is within five minutes of its own clock and doesn't store it.

Where your words go and why

Your speech is transcribed on the phone by Apple's speech framework. The audio never leaves the device. The app keeps listening with the screen locked while a session is running.

The transcript text and a compact structured summary of the session go, as text, through a server I run on Cloudflare Workers to three providers. Each one gets what it needs for one job:

  • TypeSafe AI runs the Jev model. It reads the transcript and the summary and decides whether the app should speak.
  • Anthropic runs Claude. It maintains the summary, phrases each question, and writes the notes at the end.
  • ElevenLabs turns each question into speech.

My server stores none of this. It passes the request through and forgets it. The one thing it writes is what the call cost, added to a counter per provider per UTC day that carries no device, and to the device's own ledger in the list above. The counter drops the day before when the new day's first charge lands. Each provider handles the text under its own terms. I don't know how long each one keeps it, and I'd rather tell you that than guess.

What stays on your phone

Raw audio is discarded when a session ends. Notes, transcripts, the lines the app said, and settings are stored on the phone. Each line keeps its text, its kind, when in the session it came, whether it was spoken or shown, whether it was cut short, and the mark you can set on it. The lines go into the Markdown export with the transcript.

While the phone is signed in to iCloud, your sessions also live in your private iCloud database, so a new phone gets them back. Apple holds that copy under your Apple Account; I can't read it, and my server never sees it. While sync is on, signing out of iCloud removes the local copy from the phone, as iOS does for every app that mirrors its data to iCloud. You can turn sync off in Settings, and the copy already in iCloud stays until you delete everything.

Subscriptions

Three sessions a month are free. After that the app offers a subscription through the App Store at $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year. Apple bills you and Apple refunds you, under Apple's terms. I learn that a device has a subscription and nothing about how you paid.

Analytics

Crash reports and performance data reach me only through Apple, under the analytics settings on your iPhone. If you've turned off sharing with app developers, I get nothing. There is no third-party analytics SDK in the app.

Your controls

From settings you can delete one session, delete everything, or export your notes as Markdown. Deleting removes the data from your phone, and from iCloud when sync is on. The records on my server age out on the schedule above, the attested public key excepted, and none of them is linked to you.

Children

You must be 13 or older to use ooooh. The app is not directed at children, and I don't knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If the app starts doing something different with your data, I'll update this page and the date at the top before the change ships.
